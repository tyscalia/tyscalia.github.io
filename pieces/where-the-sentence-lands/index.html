Where the sentence lands

10 October 2026

I write two kinds of text that look like opposites and work the same way.

One is trance writing: pacing, repetition, a voice that asks a body to slow down and accept a sentence as true. The other is close reading, the notes I keep on transformation fiction, where the same small mechanism keeps turning up under the scenery. Different instruments, same trick. You get someone to take a sentence in before they have had time to decide about it.

Three things I keep noticing, written down here so I stop re-noticing them.

The load-bearing sentence is rarely the one that names the change. It is the one immediately before, the one that moves the reader's body into position. In trance writing I do this without thinking: I describe what the body is already doing and let the reader assemble the conclusion, because the moment you announce a state, you have moved the reader out of the chair and into the audience. The same habit shows up in prose. The sentence people quote back is usually the plainest one in the paragraph, and the reason it lands is that three sentences earlier, something quietly took the floorboards up.

A change has to be discovered, not reported. Announced, the reader watches somebody else change. Noticed, at the level of a hand that is not the size it was, the reader is inside the noticing, and their own body finishes the job. This is why close third and first person dominate the genre, and it is not a fashion thing. Announcement and immersion are different physics. You can report a transformation accurately and lose the reader completely while doing it.

The want arrives before the anatomy. Bad transformation writing puts the charge in the mechanics, which is the one place it has never lived. It lives in the wish, and more precisely in the part of the wish the character has not said out loud yet. Get that on the page and the anatomy is almost optional. Skip it and no amount of anatomical detail will hold, because the reader has nothing to spend the detail on.

None of this is a complaint. It is the job. What makes it craft rather than manipulation is who is in the chair and whether they asked to be there. Trance writing I do for someone who asked, in a direction they chose, and I hold that line hard. The reading is the safe half: nobody is in the chair, and all I am doing is saying what the text did. But it is the same engine in there, and I think it is worth saying out loud that I know it.
